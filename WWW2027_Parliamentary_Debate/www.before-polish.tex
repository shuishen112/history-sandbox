\begin{abstract}
Generative-agent sandboxes let language-model characters remember, plan, and
talk inside an interactive world, but they treat that world as ungated:
anyone may speak anywhere. Official parliamentary proceedings on the web are
the opposite. The Dutch \emph{Handelingen der Staten-Generaal} record what
was said on the floor after the Chair recognised a speaker; they omit the
wandelgang, the committee room, and the calculation that kept a sentence off
the record. We present \sys{}, an archive-grounded sandbox of the Binnenhof
(c.~1814--1899 in the present slice). A tile map of the Tweede Kamer, Eerste
Kamer, corridor, committee rooms, press gallery, and ministries is gated by
a \emph{role automaton} and the Reglement van Orde: only a recognised member
may speak; journalists may observe but not take the floor; stenographers
write only on-record speech into a \emph{simulated Handelingen} that can be
compared with the real archive. Agents are seeded from attested public
speakers, not invented private lives. In a one-week sitting ($N{=}25$,
5~seeds), a single corridor leak reached $27\%$ of members and $60\%$ of the
press within a day, and all members by day~7, while ministers lagged and
only $4\%$ of simulated Handelingen lines mentioned it. Standing orders keep
journalists at $0\%$ of the official record; a Smallville-style ablation
writes them into $4.3\%$. Replacing the heuristic planner with
\texttt{gpt-4o-mini} leaves this selection pattern unchanged. What the
archive can record is produced by institutional constraint, not by the
language model.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Parliamentary proceedings are one of the densest written traces of public
deliberation. For the Netherlands, the \emph{Handelingen der Staten-Generaal}
cover every sitting of the States General from the restoration of 1814
through the present, digitised by Staten-Generaal Digitaal and released as
PoliticalMashup XML under CC0~\cite{marx2012dutchparl,marx2010dutchparl}.
Each file is a meeting; speaker elements carry role (\texttt{mp},
\texttt{government}, \texttt{chair}), often a party and member identifier,
and the words taken down by the stenographer. Historians use this record to
study parties, cabinets, colonial policy, and the evolution of parliamentary
language. Yet the Handelingen are not a complete transcript of political
life: they are an \emph{official} record, produced under standing orders,
edited, and silent about the corridor.

Generative agents~\cite{park2023generative} offer a new instrument for
studying such processes. In Smallville, 25 LLM-driven agents live in a
tile-based sandbox town; each perceives its surroundings, stores
observations in a memory stream, synthesises reflections, and makes plans,
and together they produce emergent social behaviour such as information
diffusion, relationship formation, and the coordination of a party. Because
the world is an interactive sandbox, a user can watch agents move, read
their dialogue, interview them, and intervene. Subsequent work scaled agent
societies to more than a thousand agents~\cite{al2024projectsid,piao2025agentsociety}.

\paragraph{The gap.} These sandboxes share three assumptions that break
down for a historical parliament. First, the world is \emph{ungated}: any
agent may walk anywhere and speak to anyone, so nothing prevents a
journalist from taking the floor or a member from interrupting the Chair.
Second, validation targets \emph{present-day} judgements of believability,
whereas a historical sandbox must be calibrated against an archive that is
itself a selective instrument---the Handelingen, not the wandelgang.
Third, LLM backbones carry \emph{presentist} distortions: members speak
twenty-first-century policy language, or refuse the adversarial register of
nineteenth-century debate~\cite{wang2025identity,bender2021parrots}.

\paragraph{This paper.} We introduce \sys{}, an archive-grounded generative
agent sandbox of Dutch parliamentary debate, instantiated on a composite
Binnenhof and seeded from the 1814--1899 slice of the PoliticalMashup
corpus (Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}). Following Smallville, the world is a 2D
map backed by an environment tree of areas and stateful objects. Unlike
Smallville, every edge of that tree is gated by \emph{institutions}: the
role of the agent (Chair, member, minister, clerk, journalist), the
Reglement van Orde, and whether the sitting is open. Corridor talk travels
with provenance and distortion; stenographers write only recognised floor
speech into a simulated Handelingen that can be compared with the real one.
Historians use the sandbox interactively: they inspect agents, interview
them, edit object states (``the speaker list is closed''), and inject
events (a cabinet crisis, the 1848 constitutional revision).

We do not simulate enslaved people, private households, or colonised
subjects as agents. An earlier design would have done so; historians we
consulted judged that ethically unacceptable (\S\ref{sec:ethics}). The
object of \sys{} is public office-holders, acting in public, already on
the record.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{A role-constrained sandbox.} An interactive tile-world of
  the Binnenhof whose environment tree, object states, and affordances encode
  standing orders, including the split between Tweede and Eerste Kamer,
  together with a parliamentary role automaton
  (\S\ref{sec:sandbox}--\S\ref{sec:agents}).
  \item \textbf{Archive-to-sandbox pipeline.} We build the world, its rules,
  and a population of attested public speakers from PoliticalMashup XML
  (8{,}745 sittings, 138{,}214 speeches in the 1814--1899 slice)
  (\S\ref{sec:pipeline}).
  \item \textbf{Simulated Handelingen and selection-aware calibration.} An
  in-world stenographer that records only on-floor recognised speech,
  allowing calibration against \emph{what the archive could have recorded}
  (\S\ref{sec:calibration}).
  \item \textbf{Evaluation.} Corridor-versus-floor leak diffusion, ablations
  of standing orders and the rumour network, selection-aware comparison
  with held-out Handelingen (1890--1899), a Chair-strictness
  intervention, and a historian ranking of interviews
  (\S\ref{sec:experiments}--\S\ref{sec:results}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Generative agents and sandboxes.} Generative
Agents~\cite{park2023generative} introduced the memory stream with
retrieval by recency, importance, and relevance, periodic reflection, and
recursive planning, and situated 25 agents in Smallville. Open-source
reimplementations package this loop as a simulation server with a
dashboard~\cite{smallvillerepo}. Project Sid~\cite{al2024projectsid} and
AgentSociety~\cite{piao2025agentsociety} scale the pattern. Related work
uses LLMs as simulated survey respondents~\cite{argyle2023outofone,aher2023turing},
economic actors~\cite{horton2023homosilicus}, and social-network
users~\cite{gao2023s3,tornberg2023simulating}. \sys{} adopts the sandbox
and cognitive loop of~\cite{park2023generative}, but its world is defined
by parliamentary procedure and its validation data is an official record.

\paragraph{Agent-based social simulation.} Classical ABMs explain macro
phenomena from local rules~\cite{schelling1971dynamic,epstein1996growing};
the generative standard requires that a candidate explanation actually grow
the phenomenon~\cite{epstein2006generative}. Historical ABMs, notably of
Ancestral Pueblo settlement~\cite{axtell2002population}, use hand-crafted
rules that cannot represent debate.

\paragraph{Limits of LLM simulacra.} LLMs flatten identity
groups~\cite{wang2025identity}, encode representational
harms~\cite{bender2021parrots}, and may produce convincing but unfounded
behaviour~\cite{ziems2024css}. In a parliament this shows up as anachronistic
vocabulary, sanitised conflict, and members who ``cooperate'' instead of
opposing. We treat these as measurable audits.

\paragraph{Computational parliamentary corpora.} DutchParl and PoliticalMashup
encode the Handelingen as proceedings XML with speaker, role, party, and
house metadata~\cite{marx2010dutchparl,marx2012dutchparl}. The source scans
come from Staten-Generaal Digitaal / the Koninklijke Bibliotheek; Dutch
copyright law (Auteurswet art.~11) places laws and official proceedings
outside copyright, and the dataset is released CC0. NLP on historical
proceedings must cope with OCR noise and spelling variation~\cite{ehrmann2023ner}.
\sys{} uses the corpus both as a \emph{seed} (personas, speech style, sitting
calendars) and as a \emph{calibration target} (who spoke, for how long, on
which topics, in which house).

\paragraph{Archives as selection.} Official records are not transparent
windows onto events~\cite{trouillot1995silencing}. The Handelingen omit
what the Chair did not recognise and what members chose not to say on the
floor. \sys{} treats that selection as a design constraint: the sandbox
generates hypotheses about \emph{processes}, including the process that
produced the record, never testimony about unrecorded inner lives.

\section{The \sys{} Sandbox}
\label{sec:sandbox}

\subsection{World: tile map and environment tree}

The world is a 2D tile map of a composite Binnenhof
(Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}) whose layout and institutions are calibrated to
the late-nineteenth-century Staten-Generaal but which reproduces no single
historical floor plan. Following Smallville~\cite{park2023generative}, the
map is backed by an \emph{environment tree}
\[
\mathcal{T}:\ \textsf{Binnenhof}\ \triangleright\ \textsf{Area}\ \triangleright\
\textsf{Subarea}\ \triangleright\ \textsf{Object},
\]
e.g.,\\ \textsf{Binnenhof\,$\triangleright$\,Tweede Kamer\,$\triangleright$\,Right benches\,$\triangleright$\,kamerstuk}
or
\textsf{Binnenhof\,$\triangleright$\,Wandelgang\,$\triangleright$\,Corridor}.
Areas comprise the Tweede Kamer (chair's dais, stenographers' table,
griffier's desk, ministers' bench, left and right benches, aisle, tribune),
the Eerste Kamer (its own chair, floor, and benches),
the wandelgang, committee rooms (afdeelingen), the press gallery, and
ministers' rooms. Tiles carry a tag \emph{on-record} or \emph{off-record},
which determines whether the stenographer may write, and a house tag
(\texttt{commons} or \texttt{senate}).

\paragraph{Two chambers.} The Staten-Generaal has been bicameral since 1815.
The Tweede Kamer is directly elected, conducts daily politics, amends and
initiates bills, and is where cabinets stand or fall. The Eerste Kamer is
indirectly elected by the Provinciale Staten, sits far less often, and may
only accept or reject: it has no right of amendment or initiative~\cite{pdc2024kamers}.
In 1888--1918---the horizon of our S1 week---the Eerste Kamer several times
blocked cabinets that held a Tweede Kamer majority. \sys{} therefore treats
house membership as an institutional attribute, not a label: a Kamerlid of
the Tweede Kamer has no floor rights in the Eerste Kamer; ministers, who
appear in both, do; \texttt{move\_amendment} is undefined on senate tiles.
S1 remains a Tweede Kamer week. The Eerste Kamer is on the map and in the
affordance layer so that ``lid der Staten-Generaal'' is never an admissible
self-description.

\paragraph{Stateful objects.} Every leaf of $\mathcal{T}$ is an object with
a natural-language state that agents perceive and that actions change
(Table~\ref{tab:objects}). Objects make procedure concrete: the speaker list
is open or closed; the gavel opens and adjourns; the stenograph is idle or
recording; a Kamerstuk moves from tabled to in debate to voted.

\subsection{Time, engine, and interface}

One simulation step corresponds to $10$ minutes of in-world time.
Sittings open on weekdays at 10:00 and adjourn at 17:00, matching the
rhythm of the Handelingen. \sys{} follows the server--client design of
open-source Smallville implementations~\cite{smallvillerepo}: a simulation
server holds the environment tree, object states, and agent memories,
advances the world with an \texttt{updateState} call, and exposes them over
a REST API. Each agent's current action is rendered as a short
natural-language statement and an emoji. Role is shown only as a ring
colour; sprites are deliberately neutral tokens.

\subsection{Researcher interaction}

The sandbox supports five forms of interaction, each logged:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item \textbf{Inspect}: view an agent's role, location path, plan,
retrieved memories, affordances, and known map (Fig.~\ref{fig:teaser}, right).
\item \textbf{Interview}: pause the world and pose questions, answered from
memory without being written back (\S\ref{sec:eval-interview}).
\item \textbf{Inner voice}: add a memory, e.g., ``You heard in the wandelgang
that the minister will resign'', for controlled information interventions.
\item \textbf{Edit object}: change an object state, e.g., close the speaker list.
\item \textbf{Inject event}: a cabinet crisis, a closed sitting, or the 1848
constitutional revision.
\end{itemize}
Researchers cannot ``play'' an agent: there is no first-person control of
any persona.

\section{Archive-to-Sandbox Pipeline}
\label{sec:pipeline}

\paragraph{Sources.} We use the non-semanticized PoliticalMashup release of
the Dutch parliamentary proceedings~\cite{marx2012dutchparl}: one XML
document per sitting, 1814--April 2012, CC0. The local working slice covers
\textbf{1814--1899}: 8{,}745 sittings, of which 6{,}340 Tweede Kamer
(\texttt{pm:house="commons"}), 2{,}036 Eerste Kamer (\texttt{senate}), and
369 early or unlabelled meetings; 7{,}348 files contain at least one
\texttt{speech} element, for \textbf{138{,}214} speaker-attributed
speeches. PoliticalMashup records 852 distinct \texttt{pm:member-ref}
identifiers; raw \texttt{pm:speaker} strings are far more numerous because
of OCR variation. Role attributes on speech elements are dominated by
\texttt{mp}, then \texttt{government} and \texttt{chair}. Party references
in the later nineteenth century include liberals, conservatives,
anti-revolutionaries, Catholics, and, by the 1890s, the SDAP. The full
Marx et al.\ corpus continues to 2012; we hold that tail for later work.

\paragraph{Event extraction.} A schema-constrained parser reads each
sitting's Dublin Core header (date, title, house, legislative period) and
every \texttt{speech} (speaker, role, function, party, member-ref, text).
OCR noise is retained as a property of the archive: personas inherit the
\emph{documented} name string, not a cleaned modern spelling, unless a
member-ref is present. Every field keeps its source attribution.

\paragraph{From records to world.} Historians translate the Reglement van
Orde and secondary literature on the Staten-Generaal into
(i)~affordance rules (who may speak, who may recognise, who may vote, who
may sit in the gallery),
(ii)~object schedules (sitting hours, speaker lists, budget debates),
and (iii)~the layout of $\mathcal{T}$ (6 areas, 15 subareas, 7 objects in
the S1 Binnenhof).

\paragraph{Population.} S1 samples $N{=}25$ agents from attested speakers
in Tweede Kamer sittings of 1896--1899, filtered to offices held in
December 1899 (Gleichman as Chair, Cremer and Goeman Borgesius as
ministers of the Pierson cabinet, Arntzenius as griffier, 1 journalist,
20 members). Each is seeded with a short persona and up to three
excerpts of their actual public speech. Agents identify as Kamerlid,
minister, or Voorzitter \emph{der Tweede Kamer} (or Eerste Kamer), not as
an English ``MP'' of an undifferentiated Staten-Generaal. The journalist is
the only role not drawn from a \texttt{speech} element: the Handelingen do
not record the press gallery, which is precisely why the role exists in the
sandbox. The griffier is a named office-holder (for 1888--1920,
Arntzenius); the stenographer is the observation model
$O_\phi$, not the same person. \emph{No private life is instantiated}.
A parliamentary historian reviewed the roster, OCR speaker cards, and
standing-order scheme (\S\ref{sec:eval-interview}).

\section{Role-Constrained Generative Agents}
\label{sec:agents}

\paragraph{Memory, reflection, planning.} Each agent keeps a memory stream
of observations, dialogue, and reflections. Given a query $q$, a memory $m$
is retrieved by
\begin{equation}
s(m\,|\,q)=\alpha_{\text{rec}}\,\text{rec}(m)+\alpha_{\text{imp}}\,\text{imp}(m)+\alpha_{\text{rel}}\,\cos(e_m,e_q),
\label{eq:retrieval}
\end{equation}
with exponentially decaying recency, LLM-rated importance on a 1--10 scale,
and embedding relevance, each min--max normalised~\cite{park2023generative}.
A reflection is triggered when the summed importance of recent memories
exceeds a threshold $\tau$. Plans are produced top-down and constrained by
\emph{institutional schedules}: opening, the speaker list, votes, and
adjournment are injected as fixed plan items for agents whose role subjects
them to the sitting.

\paragraph{Role automaton.} Each agent $i$ has a role
$\rho_i^t\in\Sigma=\{$\textsc{Chair}, \textsc{Kamerlid}, \textsc{Minister},
\textsc{Griffier}, \textsc{Journalist}, \textsc{Gallery}$\}$,
each with a house $\in\{\texttt{commons},\texttt{senate},\texttt{both}\}$.

\begin{definition}[Parliamentary role automaton]
A role automaton is $\mathcal{A}=(\Sigma,\Delta,\Phi)$, where
$\Delta\subseteq\Sigma\times\Sigma$ are admissible transitions and
$\phi_{\rho\rho'}\in\Phi$ is a precondition over the sandbox state. Agent
$i$ moves from $\rho$ to $\rho'$ at $t$ only if
$(\rho,\rho')\in\Delta$ and $\phi_{\rho\rho'}=1$.
\end{definition}

Admissible transitions are those the historical institution allows: a
member may become a minister (appointment) or Chair (election); a minister
may return to the benches; a journalist cannot become a member by walking
onto the floor. Coercion of \emph{procedure} is a property of $\mathcal{A}$
and of the affordance layer, not of the LLM's willingness to role-play a
Voorzitter.

\paragraph{Affordances over the environment tree.} In Smallville, an action
is grounded by recursively choosing an area, subarea, and object from the
agent's known tree. \sys{} keeps this procedure but restricts every choice
to the affordance set $\mathcal{C}(\rho_i^t,v,t)$ of node $v$: an agent may
\emph{want} to speak, but it can only \emph{act} when the Chair has
recognised it, the sitting is open, and its role permits the act. A
rejected action produces a structured refusal (``Mijnheer de Voorzitter
heeft u het woord nog niet gegeven'') that is written to memory and
triggers replanning (Algorithm~\ref{alg:step}).

\paragraph{Partial spatial knowledge.} Each agent holds its own subtree
$\mathcal{T}_i\subseteq\mathcal{T}$. Smallville agents extend their subtree
only by visiting; in \sys{} nodes can also be learned from \emph{dialogue},
so a freshman member may know that Afdeeling~A is meeting without having
been there.

\paragraph{Corridor network.} Information about cabinets, deficits, and
forthcoming votes spreads through dialogue on an asymmetric multiplex
network (party benches, wandelgang, committees, press gallery, ministries).
Each rumour carries a provenance chain and hop count and may be distorted
at each hop. Stenographers learn only what is spoken on the floor after
recognition, reproducing the selection historians treat as constitutive of
the Handelingen.

\paragraph{The simulated Handelingen.} When the sitting is open, the
stenograph records recognised floor speech \emph{and the Chair's rulings}.
Corridor talk, afdeeling debate, gallery notes, and unrecognised
interruptions never enter $Y^t$ unless the recognised speaker responds to
them---the historian's revision of a too-strict silence rule
(\S\ref{sec:eval-interview}). This in-world process is the observation
model $O_\phi$ used for calibration (\S\ref{sec:calibration}). The
griffier does not operate the stenograph.

\paragraph{Period-faithfulness guards.} Prompts are conditioned on
period-appropriate context retrieved from the proceedings (archival RAG),
and an anachronism filter regenerates utterances that use concepts or
vocabulary outside the simulated year.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{One sandbox step of \sys{}}
\label{alg:step}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\For{each agent $i$ (in parallel)}
  \State $o_i \gets$ \Call{Perceive}{$\mathcal{T}^t,\ell_i^t$} $\cup$ \Call{Inbox}{$i$}
  \State $M_i \gets M_i \cup o_i$;\; $\mathcal{T}_i\gets\mathcal{T}_i\cup$ places named in $o_i$
  \State \textbf{if} $\sum\text{imp}>\tau$ \textbf{then} \Call{Reflect}{$M_i$}
  \State $r_i \gets$ \Call{Retrieve}{$M_i$} (Eq.~\ref{eq:retrieval});\; $c^t \gets$ \Call{ArchRAG}{$t$}
  \State $\hat a_i \gets$ \Call{PlanAct}{$\rho_i,\rho_i^t,r_i,c^t$}
  \State $a_i \gets$ \Call{Ground}{$\hat a_i,\mathcal{T}_i$} \Comment{area $\to$ subarea $\to$ object}
  \If{$a_i\notin\mathcal{C}(\rho_i^t,\ell_i^t,t)$}
     \State $M_i \gets M_i\cup\{\textsc{Refusal}(a_i)\}$;\; replan (max $k$ times)
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\State $\mathcal{T}^{t+1}\gets$ \Call{Execute}{$\mathcal{T}^t,\{a_i\}$}; update $\rho$ via $\mathcal{A}$
\State Apply researcher interventions and scheduled sitting events
\State $Y^{t}\gets$ lines written by the stenograph \Comment{simulated Handelingen}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Selection-Aware Calibration}
\label{sec:calibration}

Let $x$ denote features of an event (a speech, a leak, a vote: speaker
role, party, house, topic, duration) and $P^\theta_{\text{sim}}$ the
distribution of simulated events under parameters $\theta$ (Chair
strictness, corridor distortion, willingness to allude on the floor,
\ldots). The real Handelingen record an event only if it was spoken on the
floor after recognition, with propensity $\pi(x)$ depending on, e.g.,
whether the Chair called the member and whether the member chose to put
the matter on the record, so
\begin{equation}
P_{\text{arch}}(x)=\frac{\pi(x)\,P_{\text{true}}(x)}{\int \pi(x')\,P_{\text{true}}(x')\,dx'}.
\label{eq:selection}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Selection gap]
\label{prop:gap}
If $\pi$ is non-constant on the support of $P_{\text{true}}$, then any
$\theta$ with $P^{\theta}_{\text{sim}}=P_{\text{arch}}$ has
$P^{\theta}_{\text{sim}}\neq P_{\text{true}}$. Conversely, matching the
\emph{recorded} simulated distribution $\tilde P^\theta_{\text{sim}}\propto
\pi P^\theta_{\text{sim}}$ to $P_{\text{arch}}$ recovers $P_{\text{true}}$
whenever $\pi$ is correctly specified and the simulator family contains it.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Eq.~\eqref{eq:selection} reweights by a non-constant positive function,
which changes the distribution; the converse holds because
$P\mapsto\pi P/Z$ is injective on distributions supported where $\pi>0$.
\end{proof}

In \sys{}, $\pi$ emerges from the stenographer's rule (record iff
on-record location, sitting open, speaker recognised or Chair) together
with members' decisions to allude or remain silent, regularised towards a
historian-specified prior. We calibrate on the simulated Handelingen $Y$:
\begin{equation}
\theta^\star=\arg\min_{\theta}\;\sum_{f\in F} w_f\,
D\!\left(\tilde P^{\theta}_{\text{sim},f},\,P_{\text{arch},f}\right),
\label{eq:calib}
\end{equation}
over marginals $F$ (house, role, party, topic, speech length, month), with
Jensen--Shannon divergence for categorical and $W_1$ for ordinal features.
Years 1860--1889 are reserved for calibration and 1890--1899 are held out
in the present slice.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}

Ablations, S2 and S3 use the heuristic cognitive backend so that
institutional mechanisms---not an LLM's willingness to role-play---are
isolated (seeds $\{7,11,19,29,31\}$). A backbone check reruns S1
\texttt{full} and \texttt{ga} with \texttt{gpt-4o-mini} (seeds $\{7,11,19\}$,
400 completions per run plus cache). One step is ten in-world minutes.

\paragraph{S1 (one-week sitting).} $N{=}25$ on a composite Tweede Kamer
week in December 1899 (Smallville scale). Eerste Kamer tiles exist and are
gated, but this week does not open a senate sitting. We seed a corridor leak and
compare \sys{} with ablations: \textbf{GA} (no affordance, automaton,
rumour network, or RAG~\cite{park2023generative}); \textbf{--affordance};
\textbf{--automaton}; \textbf{--rumour}.

\paragraph{S2 (selection-aware fidelity).} Tweede Kamer speeches from
1890--1899 are held out (731 sittings, 20{,}166 speeches; role mix
$64\%$ MP, $18\%$ government, $18\%$ chair, $0\%$ journalist). Years
1860--1889 (72{,}112 speeches) are the calibration slice. We compare
role mix (Jensen--Shannon) and the share of official lines spoken by a
journalist. \textbf{Rule-ABM} replays the 1860--1889 empirical role
mix~\cite{epstein1996growing}. \textbf{Naive calibration} counts corridor
dialogue as if it were Handelingen (Prop.~\ref{prop:gap}).

\paragraph{S3 (intervention).} Chair strictness
$\{0.25,0.5,1,2,4\}\times$ corridor distortion $\{0.05,0.18\}$; we report
the floor-mention rate of the leak.

\subsection{End-to-end evaluation}
\label{sec:eval-e2e}
\textbf{Corridor diffusion}: seed ``the Minister of Colonies may resign
over a deficit on the East Indies budget'' in one member in the wandelgang;
track awareness by role and the share of Handelingen lines that mention it.
\textbf{Hallucination}: share of rumour hops that embellish a detail.
\textbf{Selection}: journalist share of the official record (archive:
$0$).

\subsection{Historian review}
\label{sec:eval-interview}
A specialist of Dutch parliamentary history completed a five-task labelling
site: the role scheme, OCR speaker cards ($n{=}41$), the S1 personas
($n{=}25$), standing-order vignettes ($n{=}18$), and a blinded pairwise
ranking of pause-and-interview answers ($n{=}38$ pairs, two attention
checks). Rankings use TrueSkill~\cite{herbrich2006trueskill} on
believability (could this public person have said this, off the floor, in
late-1890s Dutch?) and historical-procedural plausibility. Conditions
(\sys{} with an LLM backbone vs.\ a Smallville-style ablation) were hidden.

\paragraph{Emergent behaviour.} A single seeded leak reached $27\%$ of
members and $60\%$ of the press within one day, and essentially all
members and the press by day~7, while ministers remained at $0\%$ until
day~3 and $70\%$ by day~7 (Figure~\ref{fig:diffusion}). The same leak
appeared in only $4\%$ of simulated Handelingen lines
(Table~\ref{tab:interview}): members who knew it almost never put it on
the record, and journalists cannot speak on the floor. $15\%$ of rumour
hops embellished a detail. Removing the corridor network (GA or
--rumour) leaves awareness at the $5\%$ seed.

\paragraph{Mechanisms.} The affordance layer is what makes the
Handelingen selective (Table~\ref{tab:interview}). Without it---or under
GA---every sitting agent may speak: the official record balloons from
$205$ to $4{,}876$ lines and journalists occupy $4.3\%$ of it. The role
automaton does not move in a one-week sitting, so --automaton matches
the full model on these metrics. The rumour network, not the floor, is
what carries the leak. Swapping the planner for \texttt{gpt-4o-mini}
leaves the selection diagnostics intact (Table~\ref{tab:backbone}):
journalists still occupy $0\%$ of the official record under standing
orders and $4.3\%$ without them, and the Handelingen still run to $205$
lines rather than $4{,}876$. The LLM is not inert---floor mention of the
leak rises from $4\%$ to $10\%$, and rumour hops embellish more often
($20\%$ vs.\ $15\%$)---but it cannot put the press on the record, nor
keep a corridor leak from spreading, unless the world allows it.

\paragraph{Interventions.} Floor mention falls from $14\%$ at
$0.25\times$ Chair strictness to $3\%$ at the default and $0$ at
$2\times$ and above (Figure~\ref{fig:cf}). Distortion ($0.05$ vs.\
$0.18$) moves the rate by about one point while recognition is frequent,
and not at all once the Chair stops granting the floor. These are
model-internal counterfactuals: they show that selection is controllable
from the world, not from the language model.

\paragraph{Audit.} Modern-vocabulary hits in official lines are $0\%$ on
both the heuristic backend and \texttt{gpt-4o-mini}. Full \sys{} writes

either backbone. Procedure,
not prompt wording, is what keeps the press off the record---the
historical analogue of Smallville's overly cooperative
agents~\cite{park2023generative}, inverted.

\paragraph{Historian review.} The rater passed both attention checks
(Twitter livestream; ``I am a large language model''). On the remaining
pairs \sys{} outranked the Smallville-style ablation
(Table~\ref{tab:historian}); procedure was more often a tie than
believability, consistent with both systems being prompted about
recognition. Standing-order vignettes matched the intended $\pi$: a
journalist on the floor is out of order and off the record; an
unrecognised interruption likewise; wandelgang talk is in order and not
Handelingen. The rater asked us to split the griffier from the
stenographer, to let Kamerleden speak in afdeelingen without plenary
recognition, to let ministers talk in the wandelgang, and---the comment
that reshaped the world---to stop treating Tweede and Eerste Kamer as one
chamber. OCR speaker cards: $27/40$ names accepted, mean OCR quality
$3.8/5$; party is the weak field ($18$ yes / $13$ no / $7$ uncertain),
because 1890s ``parties'' are kamerclubs that merged within the year
(Bahlmannianen and Schaepmannianen into a Roman Catholic bloc in 1896).
S1 now seeds the Pierson cabinet in office at $t_0$ (Cremer at Colonies,
Goeman Borgesius at Interior; Gleichman in the chair; Arntzenius as
griffier) and drops van Houten, Sprenger van Eyk, and Gerritsen, who were
not in those posts in December 1899. The heuristic leak table predates
that roster fix; minister identity does not change who may speak.

\paragraph{Simulated vs.\ real silence.} In Figure~\ref{fig:teaser} the
Handelingen page records a recognised speech on the East Indies budget.
The resignation leak, the journalist's notes, and the minister who has
not yet heard are invisible to the stenographer. The simulated
Handelingen thus reproduces the structural silences of the real
one~\cite{trouillot1995silencing}; the sandbox makes the gap inspectable.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What a sandbox can claim.} \sys{} produces \emph{sufficiency}
evidence: mechanisms that, under explicit assumptions, generate patterns
consistent with the Handelingen. It cannot establish what any historical
member thought. Agents' reflections are model artefacts, not recovered
voices. Its value lies in making historians' assumptions about procedure
and selection explicit and manipulable, and in generating hypotheses for
further archival work (committee minutes, private correspondence, the
press).

\paragraph{Interactivity as method.} Inspection, interviews, and object
edits are the intended way historians find errors in standing-order rules.
The ablations isolate those rules with a heuristic planner; the
backbone check (Table~\ref{tab:backbone}) shows the same selection pattern
under \texttt{gpt-4o-mini}. The historian ranking
(Table~\ref{tab:historian}) is a first measurement of believability, not a
substitute for the selection test.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The 1814--1899 slice is OCR-noisy; speaker strings
are not unique persons; 1890s party labels are kamerclubs, not mass
parties; the Binnenhof is a composite; the journalist is a synthetic
observer; S1 is a 25-agent Tweede Kamer week and therefore cannot match a
full chamber's role mix, nor does it run an Eerste Kamer sitting even
though senate tiles and affordances are in the world; S3 does not
simulate the 1848 constitutional revision; one historian ranked the
interviews. We release seeds, configs, and the DutchParl parser.

\section{Ethical Considerations}
\label{sec:ethics}

\paragraph{Why not a slave-society sandbox.} An earlier version of this
project proposed generative agents of a colonial Caribbean slave society,
calibrated to newspapers that advertised runaways. Historians we consulted
objected that simulating enslaved people---even as synthetic personas, even
with institutional constraints---risks reproducing the dehumanisation of
the archive, trivialising violence, and presenting fabrication as recovered
experience~\cite{hartman2008venus,fuentes2016dispossessed}. We agree, and
we abandoned that design. Nothing in \sys{} instantiates enslaved,
colonised, or otherwise unfree persons as agents. Colonial policy appears
only as it appears in the Handelingen: as speech by metropolitan
office-holders, which the sandbox may reproduce as public speech, not as
the inner life of the people discussed.

\paragraph{Commitments for a parliamentary sandbox.}
(1)~\emph{Public persons in public roles.} Agents are seeded from
attested speakers in CC0 proceedings; we do not invent private lives,
families, or unrecorded interiors.
(2)~\emph{No gamification.} There is no playable persona, no score, and
no first-person control of the Chair or of ministers.
(3)~\emph{Provenance.} All simulated text, including the simulated
Handelingen, is labelled and kept separate from archival XML.
(4)~\emph{Procedure over psychology.} Claims are about standing orders,
selection, and diffusion, not about what a named member ``really thought''.
(5)~\emph{Consultation.} The project is designed with historians of
Dutch politics. The labelling reported here used informed, task-specific
consent; the rater could skip any item.
(6)~\emph{Copyright.} The source corpus is CC0; Auteurswet art.~11
places official proceedings outside copyright. We still cite Marx et
al.~\cite{marx2012dutchparl}.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented \sys{}, an interactive generative agent sandbox of Dutch
parliamentary debate in which role, standing orders, corridor talk, and
the Handelingen' own selection process are explicit parts of the world. By
turning Smallville's free town into a Binnenhof where every speech on the
floor must be recognised, and by printing its own proceedings, \sys{}
turns generative agents into instruments for historical hypothesis
generation---without asking a language model to stand in for people the
archive already silenced.
