Content note. These pages quote colonial newspapers about slavery, sale, pursuit, and punishment. The people in the sandbox are invented; no labelled person is a documented historical individual.

We are building a computer simulation of a Danish West Indies island in the years before emancipation. The computer can only be as careful as the records and rules we give it. That is the part we need you for. You do not need to know how the program is written.

There are five short tasks. You can stop at any time; your answers are kept in this browser until you press Download my work at the bottom of the page, then email that file to us.

1. The scheme

Check the six kinds of newspaper notice we extract (runaway, sale, recapture, manumission, ordinance, shipping), and say which fields belong.

About 20 minutes

2. Label notices

Read computer-found passages from Dansk Vestindisk Regierings Avis and St. Croix Avis. Say what kind of notice it is, and fill the fields. Always prefer the original page over the computer’s reading.

The main task · aim for 300 notices between two readers

3. Invented people

Read a sample of sandbox characters. Flag any that sound like a real, documented person, or that misstate island society.

About 30 minutes

4. Conversations

We paused the sandbox and asked agents questions. For each pair of answers, say which is more believable, and which is more historically plausible.

About 45 minutes

5. Island rules

Confirm the map (a composite, not a real estate), the pass and curfew rules, and how often an owner would print a notice.

About 25 minutes
How to use this site
  1. Put your name in the bar at the bottom so we know whose file we received.
  2. Open a task. Click through the items. Your clicks are saved as you go.
  3. The computer’s reading of old print is full of errors. When a Royal Library link is present, open the page and trust the picture.
  4. A “suspected destination” in a runaway notice is what the advertiser claimed, not a fact about the fugitive. Mark it that way.
  5. When you stop, press Download my work and email the file. If you change computers, download first; the browser copy will not follow you.
Newspaper scans: Det Kgl. Bibliotek, Mediestream (public domain for issues older than 100 years). This site does not host the PDF files; it only stores short computer-read passages and links.
