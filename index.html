Kamer historian lab

A labelling site for the parliamentary sandbox paper. Nothing to install: work in the browser, then download a file and send it to the project.

Scope. Agents are public office-holders seeded from the CC0 Handelingen der Staten-Generaal (PoliticalMashup / Marx et al., doi:10.17026/DANS-XK5-DW3S). We do not ask you to reconstruct private lives or inner experience. There is no playable persona.

We are building a computer simulation of the Tweede Kamer in the late 1890s. The computer can only be as careful as the names, roles, and standing orders we give it. That is the part we need you for. You do not need to know how the program is written.

There are five short tasks. You can stop at any time; your answers are kept in this browser until you press Download my work at the bottom of the page, then email that file to us.

1. The scheme

Check the speaker fields we extract from DutchParl XML (name, role, party, member-ref) and the roles we allow in the sandbox.

About 15 minutes

2. Check names

Read OCR’d Handelingen excerpts. Each excerpt has its sitting date and a PDF link. Confirm the printed name, role, and whether the party is right on that date. Flag garbled readings and invented cards.

The main archival task · about 40 cards

3. Seeded people

Read the 25 agents used in the S1 week. Members and ministers should be attested speakers. Flag the journalist and clerk if they should not be named persons, or any card that is too close to a private life.

About 20 minutes

4. Conversations

We paused the sandbox and asked agents questions. For each pair of answers, say which is more believable, and which is more historically–procedurally plausible. Conditions are hidden.

About 45 minutes · TrueSkill ranking

5. Standing orders

Confirm the Binnenhof map (a composite), the Reglement van Orde rules, and whether sample lines would have been allowed and recorded.

About 25 minutes
How to use this site
  1. Put your name in the bar at the bottom so we know whose file we received.
  2. Open a task. Click through the items. Your clicks are saved as you go.
  3. OCR of nineteenth-century print is full of errors. Trust the wording of the excerpt, and correct the name if the computer misread it.
  4. A journalist has no floor rights. Corridor talk is not Handelingen. Mark procedure that way.
  5. When you stop, press Download my work and email the file. If you change computers, download first; the browser copy will not follow you.
